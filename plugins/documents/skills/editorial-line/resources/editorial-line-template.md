# Editorial line template

The order a writer applies the line in: who they write for, what is promised,
what to write about, how to write it, what never to write, in which format,
and who says yes. Each section names its source in the brief, the answers or
the identity.

```
0   Cover and record
    brand name, line version, date, status (draft | accepted),
    output language, the identity it extends: charter version and state
    (signed | drafted | none), who drafted the line, who accepts it

1   Audience
    1.1 who reads, quoted
    1.2 what they already know, what must be explained every time
    1.3 what they come for, per format if it differs
    1.4 what they misunderstand today, quoted
    1.5 where they read, and in which languages

2   Promise
    the sentence, the client's confirmation and its date, and the
    positioning it is derived from when an identity exists

3   Pillars
    three to five, each: covers, never covers, reader need, evidence,
    formats

4   Tone and vocabulary, one block per output language
    4.1 traits, "this, not that", with an example of each
    4.2 register and address
    4.3 sentences: length, order of information
    4.4 kept and refused, with reasons, sources and the word used instead
    4.5 rewrites
    4.6 mechanics: numbers, dates, prices, the brand's name

5   Never said
    5.1 claims
    5.2 comparisons
    5.3 sensitive topics, and the answer when asked
    5.4 promises, with their conditions
    5.5 sector rules, supplied by the client, marked for the adviser

6   Formats
    one specification per format the client sustains: purpose, reader,
    shape, length, cadence, who drafts; social posts as rules only, with
    the downstream named

7   Review rule
    the matrix, accepted; the per-piece record

8   Open questions and conflicts
    each with its owner and whether it blocks; every conflict with the
    identity, both sides quoted, escalated to whom and when

9   Maintenance
    owner, last verified date, the events that invalidate the line

10  Appendix
    the brief, the intake record, the client's answers quoted in full,
    the gate record
```

## Rules for the document

- A section with no decision yet says so; it is not filled with a default.
- Every client fact is quoted with its source; every gap is a visible
  marker, `[TO CONFIRM: what, held by whom]`.
- The output language is the recipient's; the structure above does not
  change with it. A second language adds a second block in section 4, not a
  translation of the first.
- `document-core` governs the delivery: the eight point gate, eleven when
  paginated, then `document-design` and `pdf-production`.
- Versioned: an accepted line is never edited in place. A change produces a
  new version, accepted again.
