# Example: an editorial line for a fictional repair co-op

**Fictional client.** Tidewell Cycle Co-op does not exist. It is the client of
the worked example of `brand-identity`
(`engineering/dev-skills/brand-identity/examples/tidewell-identity.md`), and
this example continues it. Its brief, its answers, its people and its town are
invented and labelled as such. Nothing in this line is a real fact about a
real business, and no figure appears in it, because the client supplied none.

## Where the identity stands

The identity is charter v1, **drafted and refused** at its first sign-off on
2026-10-10: glyph coverage was asserted rather than checked, and no icon
family was chosen. No second round exists yet. So, per section 3 of the skill,
its voice brief is a draft input: quoted with its version, marked unsigned,
and every point the line depends on is listed for the re-check when the
identity is signed.

What the line takes from charter v1, section 6, quoted:

```
traits      plain spoken, not casual; steady, not slow; warm, not cute;
            legible first, not decorative
does        "Your rear brake pads were worn to the metal. We replaced them."
does not    "Brakes sorted, you're good to go!"
never said  any claim of a guarantee the co-op has not stated in writing
```

## Intake

The identity intake already answered who reads, the promise, the alternative,
the languages and who accepts the brand (answers 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8 of that
example). They are reused and quoted, not asked again. Six questions remained,
sent once as one batch per `resources/intake-questions.md`. The answers, from
the fictional client:

```
E1 misread    "People think same-day means while-you-wait. It doesn't. And
              they think we'll sell them a new bike, we won't push that."
E2 conditions "In by ten, back by five. If we need a part we don't have,
              we ring you before ten the next morning with a date."
E3 never      "If someone's had a crash, we don't post about it. And
              nothing about members' private lives."
E4 written    "We say we guarantee our work. It's not written down
              anywhere."
E5 capacity   "One of us could write something once a month. No newsletter,
              nobody reads those. We post on our social account now and
              then."
E6 tone       "On social we'd like it more fun, like 'Brakes sorted, you're
              good to go!'. People like that."
```

```
EDITORIAL INTAKE   Tidewell (fictional), 2026-10-10

Identity          drafted, charter v1, refused round 1
Audience          answered   identity answer 1, E1
Offer             answered   identity answer 2, E2
Constraints       answered   E3, E4, E5
Assumptions       article length 400 to 700 words, not blocking
Verdict           proceed, with E4 and E6 as open questions
```

E4 is a promise with no document behind it, so it is a gap, not a claim. E6
contradicts the identity's voice brief word for word. Both are handled below,
and neither is settled by this skill.

## The line, version 1 (draft)

Output language: English, from identity answer 6. Readers of this document:
whoever writes for the co-op, the members first.

### 1. Audience

```
who reads       "People who ride to work and can't be without the bike for
                a week. A lot of them are older. Some have never had a bike
                serviced before."                     (identity answer 1)
what they know  how to ride; not the names of parts. Every part is named
                with what it does, the first time in each piece.
what they need  to know when the bike is back, and what was done to it
what they misread  same-day is not while-you-wait; the co-op does not push
                a new bike                                         (E1)
where           a phone, often; the counter and the receipts
languages       English only
```

No age figure, share or count of riders is given. "A lot of them are older"
is the client's statement, quoted as one.

### 2. Promise

```
Riders who depend on their bike learn what was wrong with it, what was done,
and when it will be back, because the co-op explains its work at the
counter and in writing, in words a first-time customer follows.
```

Derived from the positioning of charter v1, which says why choose Tidewell.
This sentence says why read it. Confirmation by the co-op secretary:
`[TO CONFIRM: promise wording, held by the co-op secretary]`.

### 3. Pillars

| Pillar | Covers | Never covers | Need | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What we found | a fault seen in the workshop, explained after the repair, with the part and why it failed | a step by step repair of brakes, wheels or steering: work a rider must not attempt alone | what was done, and why | identity answer 2, "we show you what we did and why" |
| Keeping it rolling | care a rider does at home: tyre pressure, a dry chain, lights | anything on brakes or bearings; any line suggesting the rider will not need the workshop | fewer surprises | identity answer 1, first-time customers |
| How the workshop works | the ten o'clock cut-off, what happens when a part is ordered, what to bring, the counter | prices not supplied by the co-op; members' private lives; the chain store | when the bike is back | E1, E2, E3 |

Three pillars: the co-op can write one piece a month (E5), and three lets each
pillar return once a quarter.

### 4. Tone and vocabulary, English

Traits from charter v1, applied to words. The examples are the identity's;
the rule for social posts is left out, see section 8.

```
register        "we" for the co-op, "you" for the rider; never the co-op's
                name in the third person in running text
sentences       short; what happened, then what we did, then what you do
mechanics       times as "10am" and "5pm"; the co-op's name in full,
                "Tidewell", never abbreviated
```

| Kept | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| rider | the client's own word for the reader | identity answer 1 |
| we show you | the practice behind the promise | identity answer 2 |
| worn, replaced, adjusted | what was done, said plainly | charter v1, "plain spoken" |
| back by 5pm | the condition stated, not "same day" alone | E2 |

| Refused | Reason | Instead | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| sorted, good to go | casual, the identity's "does not" example | say what was done | charter v1, draft |
| guaranteed, guarantee | no written guarantee exists | nothing, until E4 is closed | E4, charter v1 never said |
| same day, alone | read as while-you-wait | back by 5pm if in by 10am | E1, E2 |
| upgrade, new bike | the co-op does not push a sale | name the part replaced | E1 |
| just, simply, easily | `document-core` section 6 | the steps themselves | the tree's style standard |

Rewrites:

| Refused | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| sorted | Brakes sorted! | Your rear brake pads were worn to the metal. We replaced them. |
| same day | Same-day repairs. | Bring it in by 10am and it is back by 5pm. If we need a part, we ring you before 10am the next morning with a date. |
| guaranteed | All work guaranteed. | `[TO CONFIRM: the guarantee terms, in writing, held by the co-op secretary]` until supplied; until then the sentence is not written |
| just | Just pump your tyres weekly. | Check your tyre pressure once a week. The number is printed on the side of the tyre. |

### 5. Never said

| List | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Claims | no guarantee until the written terms exist; no "best", "fastest" or "cheapest" | E4, charter v1 |
| Comparisons | the chain store at the retail park is never described or alluded to, even though the client described it in the identity intake | identity answer 3 |
| Sensitive topics | no post or article about a crash or an injury, even a customer's own; nothing about members' private lives. Proposed answer when asked about a crash, to be confirmed with Q2: "We can look at the bike. We don't talk about what happened." | E3 |
| Promises | same day is written only with "in by 10am" and the part-order condition | E2 |

### 6. Formats

| Format | Purpose | Shape | Length | Cadence | Downstream |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| site pages: home, how a repair works, visit us | when the bike is back, what was done | the promise first, then the cut-off, then the counter | home under 150 words; others under 400 | updated when the offer changes | `site-template-generation` content file |
| article | one pillar per piece | the fault, the part, why, what the rider can watch for | 400 to 700 words, assumed, not blocking | at most monthly (E5) | the site's articles page |
| social post | rules only: one pillar, the vocabulary, the never-said list | not specified here | not specified here | "now and then" (E5) | the social-content skill, planned |

No newsletter is specified: the client said it will not sustain one (E5).

### 7. Review rule

| Piece | Drafter | Reviewer | Approver | Also required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| site page | the drafting agent or a member | a member who did not draft | the co-op secretary | |
| article | the drafting agent or a member | a member who did not draft | the co-op secretary | the member who did the repair confirms what was found |
| social post | a member | another member | the co-op secretary | |
| any claim, such as a guarantee | | | the co-op secretary | the written terms |
| a member named or photographed | | | the co-op secretary | that member's consent, recorded |

The secretary publishes, or names who does. The agent drafts and hands over;
it never publishes and never approves its own draft.

### 8. Open questions and conflicts

```
C1  conflict, blocking for social posts only
    client (E6)      "more fun, like 'Brakes sorted, you're good to go!'"
    identity         charter v1 voice brief: that exact sentence as "does
                     not"; trait "plain spoken, not casual"
    action           escalated 2026-10-10 to the design-director agent and
                     to the co-op secretary, who accepts the brand
    in the line      no register rule for social posts until decided; the
                     identity's refusal of "sorted" stands for site pages
                     and articles, which the client did not contest
    if decided for   the identity must change: back through brand-identity
    the client       and a new sign-off; this skill does not edit a charter

Q1  guarantee terms, blocking any guarantee claim       co-op secretary (E4)
Q2  promise wording, and the proposed answer about a     co-op secretary
    crash, confirmed
Q3  re-check against the identity when it is signed     drafting agent
```

owner             the co-op secretary (fictional)

## Quality gate

```
Document: Tidewell editorial line, v1 draft, English
Audience: writers for the co-op, members first; describing riders who
          depend on their bike
1 Content     pass  every client fact quoted with its answer; E4, the
                    promise wording and C1 marked
2 Structure   pass  the template order; a writer finds the refused list
                    without reading section 1
3 Language    pass  English throughout; "rider" used for the reader
                    everywhere
4 Formatting  pass  three heading levels; not paginated, so 9 to 11 do
                    not apply
5 Audience    pass  written for the writer: rules and rewrites, not a
                    description of the brand
6 Consistency pass  "10am", "5pm", "Tidewell" identical throughout
7 Requirement pass  all seven parts present; no newsletter, by the
                    client's answer
8 Self critique  pass  professional editor, client, end user roles
Gaps remaining: Q1, Q2 (co-op secretary); C1 (design-director agent and
                co-op secretary); Q3 on signature of the identity
Status: draft. Not delivered as final while C1 is open.
```

## What the example shows

- The identity's state was read first. Because it is drafted and refused,
  its voice brief was used as a draft input, quoted with its version, and
  the line names the re-check its signature will trigger.
- Answers already given to the identity were reused, not asked twice; the
  six that remained went out once, as one batch.
- No figure, testimonial or competitor appears. The guarantee the client
  states but never wrote down is a gap, and the sentence that needs it is not
  written.
- The client's wish for a casual social voice contradicts the identity word
  for word. It was escalated to the design-director agent and the client,
  left out of the line, and not settled by this skill.
- The newsletter the client will not sustain was not specified. Social posts
  get the line's rules only, with the downstream skill named.
- The gate passed for a draft, and the draft is not called final while a
  conflict is open.
