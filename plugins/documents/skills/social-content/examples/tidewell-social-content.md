# Example: social content for a fictional repair co-op

**Fictional client.** Tidewell Cycle Co-op does not exist. It is the client of
the worked examples of `brand-identity`
(`engineering/dev-skills/brand-identity/examples/tidewell-identity.md`) and
`editorial-line`
(`documents/communication/editorial-line/examples/tidewell-editorial-line.md`),
and this example continues them. Its answers, its people, its account and
its town are invented and labelled as such. No figure about an audience, a
reach or a result appears, because none exists, and no network specification
is stated, because none was read for a fictional account.

## Where the inputs stand

Read first, per section 3 of the skill, and recorded on the cover:

```
editorial line   v1, draft. Conflict C1 open: the client wants a casual
                 social voice ("Brakes sorted, you're good to go!"); the
                 identity's voice brief refuses that sentence word for word.
                 The line holds no register rule for social posts until the
                 design-director agent and the co-op secretary decide.
                 Q1 (guarantee terms) and Q2 (promise wording) open.
identity         charter v1, drafted and refused at round 1 (glyph coverage
                 asserted, no icon family). No round 2 yet.
```

Consequences, from the table in section 3:

- Every post is **provisional** at best: its register depends on C1, which
  no post may settle.
- Every visual is a draft against a refused charter; nothing visual is
  handed over as final.
- No caption mentions a guarantee (Q1), and none paraphrases the promise
  (Q2).

The posts below are written in the register the line does state for site
pages and articles, plain spoken, because that is the only register the line
holds. They are not an attempt at the casual voice, and not an argument
against it: whichever way C1 is decided, they are re-checked.

## Intake

The line's intake already answered the audience (identity answer 1, E1), the
offer and its conditions (E2), the subjects refused (E3) and the capacity in
general terms (E5: "We post on our social account now and then"). Those are
quoted, not asked again. Four questions remained, sent once as one batch per
`resources/channel-calendar.md`. The answers, from the fictional client:

```
S1 channels  "Just the one account, on the photo app. Mara has the login.
             Nobody's posted since the spring. We don't want any others."
S2 capacity  "Dev takes photos in the workshop on Tuesdays when it's quiet,
             an hour at most. Mara can look at comments at lunch on
             weekdays. Nobody on Sundays, and the workshop shuts the last
             two weeks of December."
S3 replies   "Mara answers everything. If it's a complaint, Mara decides.
             If anyone mentions a crash or a solicitor, Mara rings Sol, who
             chairs the co-op, before anyone says anything. A journalist,
             same, Sol."
S4 measure   "We'd like to know if anyone comes in because of it."
```

```
SOCIAL INTAKE   Tidewell (fictional), 2026-10-10

Line              v1 draft, C1 open (blocking the social register)
Identity          charter v1, refused round 1
Channels          answered   S1
Capacity          answered   S2, E5
Reply authority   answered   S3
Assumptions       posts on Wednesdays, from Tuesday's photographs; not
                  blocking
Verdict           proceed with drafts, all provisional; nothing handed over
                  as ready while C1 is open
```

## The plan, version 1 (provisional)

Output language: English, from the line. Readers: Mara, who holds the
account, and Dev, who takes the photographs (both fictional).

### 1. Channels

| Channel | Why, from the line | Holder | Kept or left out |
|---|---|---|---|
| the co-op's existing account on a photo-sharing network (fictional) | riders "on a phone, often" (line, audience); the pillar "What we found" is visual | Mara | kept (S1) |
| any other network | none | | left out: the client does not want one (S1), and capacity covers one channel (S2) |

### 2. Cadence, derived

```
CADENCE   photo-sharing account, 2026-10-10

Capacity          Dev, one hour on Tuesdays for photographs; Mara at
                  weekday lunch for comments; nobody on Sundays (S2)
Cost per post     photograph and selection: most of Dev's hour; caption
                  drafted by the agent; review by a member who did not
                  draft; approval by Mara; replies at Mara's lunches.
                  Estimated with Mara: one post fits in a fortnight
                  without taking her lunches for a week.
Cadence           one post every two weeks
Three-month test  yes, by Mara's estimate; weekly was proposed and refused
                  by Mara, because a post brings questions she answers
                  herself
```

No "post every day". One post a fortnight is what the client said it can
hold, and the plan holds it.

### 3. Calendar, first period

Pillars from the line, rotated so that each returns every six weeks.

| Date | Pillar | Format | Brief | Drafter | Approver | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wed 2026-10-21 | How the workshop works | one photograph, caption | P1 | the drafting agent | Mara | provisional (C1) |
| Wed 2026-11-04 | What we found | one photograph, caption | P2 | the drafting agent | Mara | held (consent, confirmation, C1) |
| Wed 2026-11-18 | Keeping it rolling | one photograph, caption | P3 | the drafting agent | Mara | provisional (C1) |

The December closure (S2) is the client's date; the period after it is
planned once the closure dates are confirmed:
`[TO CONFIRM: closing and reopening dates, held by Mara]`.

### 4. Format sheet

| Network | Format | Specification | Checked on | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| photo-sharing (fictional) | single image post | `[TO CHECK at publication: image ratio and size, caption length limit]` | not read | the network's own help pages, read on the handover day |
| photo-sharing (fictional) | alt text | `[TO CHECK at publication: where the publishing tool takes alt text]` | not read | the network's own help pages |

No value is stated. The account is fictional, so no page was read; in a real
run the network's own help or developer page is read, its URL and the date
recorded, before the handover. The photographs are drafted at a ratio the
identity's imagery direction allows, and the handover tells Mara what to
check in the app on the day.

### 5. Posts

**P1, How the workshop works. Provisional (C1).**

```
Reader need     "when the bike is back" (line, audience)
The one thing   in by 10am, back by 5pm; if a part is needed, a call
                before 10am the next morning
Sources         E2, the client's own words; nothing else stated
Consents        none needed: the counter clock and the drop-off rack, no
                person, no customer's bike identifiable
Visual          Dev's photograph of the drop-off rack at 10am (R1 of the
                identity: the co-op's own photographs)
Caption         Bring your bike in by 10am and it is back by 5pm. If we
                need a part we don't have, we ring you before 10am the
                next morning with a date.
Alt text        The drop-off rack at the Tidewell counter, with the wall
                clock showing ten o'clock.
Reply plan      questions about Saturdays: Mara, at lunch;
                [TO CONFIRM: Saturday opening hours, held by Mara]
```

The caption is the line's own rewrite of "same day", unchanged. "Same day"
alone is refused by the line and does not appear.

**P2, What we found. Held.**

```
Reader need     "what was done, and why"
The one thing   a rear brake pad worn to the metal, and what a rider hears
                before it gets there
Sources         the member who did the repair confirms the fault
                [TO CONFIRM: the repair and the part, held by the member
                who did it]
Consents        the bike belongs to a customer. Its owner's consent to show
                it is required by the line's review rule
                [TO CONFIRM: owner's consent, recorded by Mara]
Visual          Dev's close photograph of the worn pad beside a new one;
                no frame, no sticker, nothing that identifies the bike
Caption         The rear brake pad on the left was worn to the metal. The
                one on the right is new. Before it got this far, the brake
                would have squealed, then scraped.
Alt text        Two brake pads side by side: the left one worn down to its
                metal backing, the right one new.
Reply plan      "can I change my own pads": the pillar never covers a
                brake repair a rider must not attempt alone; Mara answers
                with the counter, never with steps
```

Held for three reasons: the member's confirmation, the owner's consent, and
C1. The line's own example, "Your rear brake pads were worn to the metal. We
replaced them", is addressed to the bike's owner; on a public account it
would describe a customer's bike to strangers, so the caption speaks about
the part, not the person.

**P3, Keeping it rolling. Provisional (C1).**

```
Reader need     "fewer surprises"
The one thing   check tyre pressure once a week; the number is on the tyre
Sources         the line's rewrite of "just"
Consents        none: a tyre wall, no person
Visual          Dev's photograph of a tyre's sidewall with the pressure
                marking in focus
Caption         Check your tyre pressure once a week. The number is printed
                on the side of the tyre.
Alt text        Close-up of a bicycle tyre's sidewall, showing the
                recommended pressure printed in the rubber.
Reply plan      "which pump": Mara answers that the counter can show you;
                no brand is recommended
```

Caption checklist run on all three: pillar named, no refused word, no claim,
no comparison, no guarantee, no figure about anyone. P2 fails "every person
shown has a recorded consent" until the owner's consent is recorded; it stays
held.

### 6. Visuals

Drafted as files in the co-op's folder, beside each brief; no canvas was
used. The photographs are the co-op's own (R1), with the co-op's permission
recorded in the identity's intake. The charter is refused at round 1, so each
visual is labelled a draft against charter v1.

```
V1  visual question, sent 2026-10-10 to the design-director agent
    Mara asks for the wordmark in the corner of every photograph.
    Charter v1 misuse list: "placed on a photograph without the reversed
    version". No reversed version exists yet, and the charter is refused.
    Not decided by the drafter: until the design-director agent decides,
    the photographs carry no mark.
```

### 7. Moderation and replies

```
reply authority   Mara (S3)
complaints        Mara decides (S3)
press             Sol, chair of the co-op (S3, fictional)
legal, safety     Mara rings Sol before anyone replies (S3)
hours             weekday lunchtimes; nobody on Sundays (S2)
```

| Kind | Answered? | By whom | Within | How | Escalated to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| question about the offer | yes | Mara | by the next weekday lunch (S2) | public, from the line | |
| praise | yes | Mara | by the next weekday lunch | public, short | |
| criticism, fair | yes | Mara | by the next weekday lunch | public, acknowledged, no argument | |
| complaint: work, money | escalated | Mara decides | by the next weekday lunch | moved to private | Mara |
| a crash, an injury | escalated | never the drafter | Mara rings Sol at once | no public reply until Sol decides; the line's proposed answer, "We can look at the bike. We don't talk about what happened.", is pending Q2 | Sol |
| a solicitor, a claim | escalated | never the drafter | at once | no public reply | Sol, then the co-op's adviser |
| a journalist | escalated | | | | Sol |
| a comment about the chain store | yes, without the comparison | Mara | by the next weekday lunch | the co-op describes itself only (line, comparisons) | |
| spam, abuse | no | Mara | | hidden, recorded | |
| personal data in public | escalated | Mara | at once | hidden if the app allows, private reply | |

Never done: delete a fair complaint about a wait; argue about prices; name a
customer whose bike is in the workshop; promise a guarantee in a reply (Q1).

### 8. Measurement

| Question (S4) | Signal | Source | Can attribute | Cannot attribute | Baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| does anyone come in because of it? | at drop-off, Mara asks "how did you hear about us?" and ticks a box | the co-op's drop-off book | people who say a post brought them | people who saw a post and did not say so, or came weeks later | none yet |
| is anyone seeing the posts? | followers, likes | the network's own analytics, its definitions read on the day | that accounts reacted, by the network's definition | that they are riders, customers, or local; **vanity metrics**, reported as such | none yet |

No target is set: the client gave none. No tagged link is planned, because
the co-op's site does not exist yet, and any tracking would go through
`analytics-instrumentation` and `data-privacy` first.

## What the example shows

- The states of the line and the identity were read first. With C1 open and
  the charter refused, every post is provisional or held, and the plan says
  so on its cover and at the top of the handover.
- The posts use the only register the line states. None tries the casual
  voice "to see how it does": that would settle C1 in public.
- The cadence came from Dev's hour and Mara's lunches, not from a rule. A
  weekly cadence was proposed and refused by the client, and the refusal is
  recorded.
- No network specification is stated: none was read, so each is a field with
  where to check it on the day.
- A customer's bike is a person's data. P2 waits for the owner's consent and
  speaks about the part, not the customer.
- The wordmark on photographs is a visual question the charter does not
  settle, sent to the design-director agent and not decided by the drafter.
- Crashes, solicitors and journalists go to the person the client named,
  before anyone replies. Measurement asks the client's own question at the
  counter and labels followers and likes as vanity metrics, with no target
  invented.
- Nothing is published, scheduled or replied to by the agent. Mara posts.
